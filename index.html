A child's path at FMS

From first steps to leading the room.

  1. 15 mo

    Young Toddler begins

    A room that feels like an extension of home. Walking, carrying, greeting — the first practical life work.

  2. 2–4

    Pre-Primary

    Sensorial materials, songs and stories, and early math. No potty training required to start.

  3. 3

    Primary begins

    The three-year cycle starts, with a long morning work period and every material on the shelf available.

  4. 4–5

    Language & math deepen

    Sandpaper Letters become words; Golden Beads open the door to multiplication, division and fractions.

  5. 5–6

    The Year of Leadership

    Kindergarten year: reading, writing and math put to work — and younger classmates mentored along the way.

  6. 5:30

    After school

    Pre-Primary and Primary children can stay for crafts, music and dance until 5:30 pm.

  7. Breaks

    Winter, spring & summer camp

    For enrolled students, 8:30 am to 4:00 pm, with weekly themes, gardening and a weekly summer water day.

    Where it all began

    A method with more than a century behind it.

    Maria Montessori was among the first women to become a physician in Italy — but her real passion was education.

    1. 1870

      Maria Montessori is born on August 31 in Chiaravalle, Italy.

    2. 1907

      She opens the first Casa dei Bambini — the "Children's House" — in Rome.

    3. Books

      The Montessori Method and The Secret of Childhood carry her ideas worldwide.

    4. 1952

      She dies on May 6 in Noordwijk, Netherlands. Her schools keep growing.

    5. 1967

      Flossmoor Montessori School opens its doors on Western Avenue.

    Questions

    What parents ask us first.

    What ages do you take?

    Children from about 15 months to 6 years, across three programs: Young Toddler (15–30 months), Pre-Primary (2–4 years) and Primary (3–6 years), with kindergarten as the final Primary year.

    Does my child need to be potty trained?

    Not for Pre-Primary. The program supports children as they build self-care skills, toileting included.

    Does Montessori work for every child?

    A skilled Guide can adapt Montessori to most children. We accept children with special needs selectively, so every classroom keeps working well for everyone in it.

    Why mix ages in one classroom?

    Mixed ages build leadership, empathy, patience and responsibility. Older children aren't required to help younger ones — but they often choose to, and teaching reinforces their own learning.

    What if my child avoids hard work?

    Materials are arranged by difficulty and Guides watch each child closely, nudging the hesitant and challenging the bold. Children move on as they master each step.

    Do children learn music and art?

    Yes. Art, music, dance and literature are available throughout the day, along with flower arranging and paper folding. Children choose when to engage.

    How do tours work?

    Tours are by appointment. We also host an adults-only tour with a parent volunteer and our administrative staff on the second Tuesday of each month, starting promptly at 10 a.m.

    What are your hours?

    The office is open 8:00 am to 3:00 pm. After-school care runs until 5:30 pm, Monday to Friday, and camps run 8:30 am to 4:00 pm during winter, spring and summer breaks.
